# Present visual identity

**Authoritative direction for Phases 4–7 and future present-day scenes:** Modern Illuminated Papercraft Diorama. The present is a modern festival in the same illustrated storybook universe as the past. It changes materials, color, architecture, technology, scale and density; it keeps layered silhouettes, 2.5D depth, controlled parallax and theatrical composition.

## Visual grammar

- Compose each camera shot before adding detail. Identify its focal point, foreground frame, midground action, background, light path and dark breathing space.
- Use full 3D for hero and interactive objects: player lanterns, Tháp Đèn Kéo Quân, Inner Gate, close Elder or Moon Guardian, puzzle props and nearby structures the camera approaches.
- Use shallow geometry, cut-paper shapes, layered planes and billboard groups for most environmental storytelling: crowds, foliage, secondary stalls, distant buildings, decorative architecture and skyline.
- Design clear contours, varied posture, intentional overlap, negative space and asymmetry. Judge an object as a silhouette first.
- Move depth layers with restrained parallax: near layers move more than far layers. Avoid the look of independently sliding flat images.

## People and environment

- **Crowd:** a few stylized hero characters near camera; reusable 2.5D people and small groups in the middle distance; silhouette sheets or instanced light figures behind them; distant lanterns and warm light points at the horizon. The crowd should feel illustrated and alive through small shifts, lantern sway and varied silhouettes.
- **Foliage:** use designed trunks, overlapping foliage cards and cut-paper canopy masses. Dark foreground branches may frame the camera. Thin, atmospheric layers describe distant trees and bamboo.
- **Architecture:** give modern structures illustrated façades, shallow rooflines, translucent panels, layered signage, warm windows, fabric banners and elegant thin frames. Their silhouette and façade carry the identity; volume supports it.
- **Shape language:** thin arcs, soft trapezoids, lantern circles, stars, crescents, narrow frames, light ribbons and floating paper panels.

## Material, color and light

- Keep midnight navy and deep blue as the night field. Use warm gold, coral and crimson for festival lights, with restrained soft cyan, muted purple and emerald. Preserve Vietnamese Mid-Autumn motifs, especially the star lantern.
- Present-day materials may include translucent lantern paper, frosted acrylic, painted or brushed metal, fabric, lacquered wood, matte stone and subtle glass. Electric light should shine through and around these forms.
- Paint light in a rhythm of warm pools, cool gaps, dark space, backlight and rim light. Hero lanterns lead the eye; generic path LEDs do not dominate them.
- Use haze, mist, softer contrast and reduced saturation with distance. Keep near silhouettes crisp and distant scenery quiet; allow selected warm lights to shine through.
- Keep scenes alive with small authored motion: lantern sway, cloth movement, head turns, crowd idle motion, light patterns and drifting haze.

## Past and present

The past is handmade, matte and intimate: bamboo, paper, cloth, candlelight and moonlit mist. The present is cleaner, richer and more electric: translucent paper, acrylic, LED accents, festival architecture and larger crowds. **The world changed; the storybook language remained.** Do not copy village assets literally into the modern festival.

## Approval check

Before approving a present-day shot, inspect it in the running game. Check: illustrated diorama feel; intentional silhouettes; foreground, middle and background layers; authored lighting and atmosphere; modern light interacting with paper-like materials; visually designed people, trees and façades; controlled camera composition; and Vietnamese Mid-Autumn identity.

Capsule people, generic box buildings, cone trees, sphere/blob canopies and other primitive meshes are temporary greybox assets unless deliberately transformed into designed forms. Do not interpret “stylized 3D” as permission to replace the diorama with a conventional low-poly level. When a scene drifts, favor illustration, layering, silhouette and staging over volumetric complexity.

This document defines future visual work. It does not itself authorize reconstruction of existing Phase 4–6 scenes. The next Phase 7 implementation should use this direction from its first composition pass.
